## D-DSR (2026-11-02, Tony): the same reviewer settles the dispute when there is only one reviewer
Tony, 2026-11-02 13:15 BRT: "Let the same reviewer settle the dispute when there's only one reviewer."
1. **Eligible reviewers of a record** are the people the Worker lists as reviewers or admins (`REVIEWER_EMAILS`, `ADMIN_EMAILS`, Access mode) plus the resolved N+1 of the record, never its owner. In Entra mode (app-role holders cannot be listed by the Worker) or without the Worker's variables the count is unknown, and an unknown count is never treated as one: the four-eyes rule applies.
2. **Exactly one eligible reviewer:** that reviewer may uphold or overturn (`uphold_rejection`, `reopen_review`) the dispute of their own rejection. **Two or more:** unchanged, the rejecter cannot adjudicate (`ADJUDICATOR_IS_REJECTER`, 403). The owner still never adjudicates (`SELF_APPROVAL`), and an adjudicator named by the owner stays recorded, not enforced (D-DSP 3).
3. **Recorded as a single-reviewer adjudication:** `adjudication.rule = 'single_reviewer'` and `adjudication.single_reviewer = true` on the record, the code `single_reviewer_adjudication` on the `workflow_transitions` row (beside `text_recorded`, never the text), and an entry with the same action in the hash-chained audit. Four-eyes decisions carry `rule = 'four_eyes'`; the owner's acceptance carries `rule = 'owner'`.
4. **Reviewer screen:** a disputed record waits in the review queue with a Disputed chip (not counted for a rejecter who cannot settle it). The one-screen review shows the rejection and the dispute side by side and Uphold the rejection / Overturn and review again, each with a required reason; the owner raises a dispute (reason, optional adjudicator) or accepts the rejection from the status panel. Buttons are enabled only from the record's `actions`; a disabled one says why next to it.
