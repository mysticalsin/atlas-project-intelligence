# Atlas Governance, Privacy & Approval Model

## 1. Core controls
- Least privilege by role, project, country and purpose.
- Stable identity mapping; do not use email as the only identity key.
- Data minimization and retention schedule by category.
- Separate private interview session content from validated organizational evidence.
- Access logs for sensitive records and every approval/write.
- Versioned correction, dispute, withdrawal and deletion workflows.
- Country-specific legal and HR requirements are configuration and require local owner approval.

## 2. Purpose separation
Four independent permissions:
1. Internal organisational knowledge.
2. Consultant CV use.
3. Bid/client-facing use.
4. External/public Spotlight publication.

A user may authorize one and deny another. Permissions must bind to content scope and revision. Expiry and revocation must be supported.

## 3. Human decision boundaries
Atlas may assist with preparation and evidence organization. It must not autonomously decide employment, compensation, promotion, termination, formal performance rating or disciplinary outcomes. MAAP decisions remain with authorized people using the approved process.

## 4. Spotlight release controls
No publication without:
- claim-level evidence mapping;
- project/account factual validation;
- fair contribution attribution;
- customer permission where required;
- confidentiality/privacy/legal/brand review;
- explicit approval of exact revision and channel.

## 5. Sensitive information
Do not infer or classify health, race, ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, political affiliation, union membership or other sensitive traits. Do not infer mental state, attrition likelihood or performance from vocal tone, accent, facial expression or sentiment. Restrict people-review data to approved roles and purposes.

## 6. AI transparency
Tell users when Atlas is drafting, extracting or proposing. Distinguish source facts from generated summaries. Explain uncertainty. Do not expose hidden reasoning; provide concise rationale, evidence references and policy outcome.

## 7. Incident handling
If Atlas detects an unauthorized disclosure, incorrect publication, wrong-person match or unintended write:
1. stop further processing/publication;
2. preserve audit details;
3. notify designated owner through approved incident path;
4. identify affected records and derivatives;
5. correct, revoke or unpublish where authorized;
6. record resolution and preventive action.
