# Spotlight — Success Story Workflow

## 1. Purpose
Spotlight is the named destination for validated, approved success stories. In Atlas architecture, replace “Publication” with **“Spotlight — Success Stories”**. It is not a generic data-publication sink.

## 2. Story structure
- Customer context (named or anonymised according to permission).
- Business challenge and starting point.
- Project scope and objective.
- Consultant/team contribution and role attribution.
- Approach and key decisions.
- Technologies and methods.
- Deliverables.
- Outcomes, metrics, measurement period and evidence.
- Lessons learned and reusable capability.
- Approved call to action and publication metadata.

## 3. Story lifecycle
Candidate → Draft → Evidence check → Project/account validation → Customer permission (where required) → Confidentiality/privacy/legal/brand review → Exact-revision approval → Scheduled → Published → Updated/Withdrawn.

## 4. Approval gates
1. Factual accuracy: every material claim maps to approved evidence.
2. Attribution: consultant/team contribution is fair and specific.
3. Customer permission: customer naming, logo, quote and metric permissions are separately recorded.
4. Confidentiality: no restricted architecture, data, pricing, personal information or commercially sensitive details.
5. Privacy/legal: required country/legal checks completed.
6. Brand/comms: wording, visual assets, claims and CTA approved.
7. Publication authorization: named approver approves the exact story revision and channels.

## 5. Permission matrix
- Internal knowledge reuse.
- Consultant CV use.
- Bid/client-facing use.
- External/public publication.
These permissions are independent and may have expiry, scope, territory or channel limitations.

## 6. JEV and story relevance
JEV may identify that an existing story is relevant to a prospect, industry, challenge or technology. Relevance is a retrieval/ranking signal only. It does not validate facts, grant permission or authorize publication. Story ranking must explain matched criteria and preserve the original approved content.

## 7. Correction and withdrawal
A correction creates a new revision. Identify derivative CVs, proposals, presentations, webpages and campaign assets. Depending on the reason, suspend distribution, update, unpublish or request takedown. Record who requested it, why, when, affected derivatives and completion.

## 8. Measurement
Track story creation time, approval cycle, publication channels, views/engagement where available, qualified inquiries, opportunities influenced and evidence of reuse. Do not claim revenue causality from simple association.
